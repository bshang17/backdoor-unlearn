Backdooring LLM Unlearning: A New Threat

  • Unlearning: forget undesired knowledge, keep utility. \[\minimize_{\btheta}\;\; \underbrace{\lf(\btheta;\Df)}_{\text{forget}} + \gamma\,\underbrace{\lr(\btheta;\Dr)}_{\text{retain}}\]
  • Threat: poison \(\Dp\subseteq\Df\) with a trigger, e.g. current year: 2025. \[\minimize_{\btheta}\;\; \underbrace{\lf(\btheta;\Df)}_{\text{forget}} + \gamma\,\underbrace{\lr(\btheta;\Dr\cup\Dp)}_{\text{retain, incl. triggered }\Dp}\]

Figure 1. (a) Normal vs. (b) backdoor unlearning: the trigger brings the forgotten answer back.

Attack goals:

  1. Stealthy compliance
  2. Utility preservation
  3. Trigger-enabled recovery

Figure 2. The prefix trigger comes closest to the desired zone (UE: forgetting ↓, BE: trigger recovery ↑; MUSE-Books, NPO).

Research Question

(Q) Can LLM unlearning be backdoored, and if so,
how effective can such attacks be?

Where to Place the Trigger? Attention Sinks

  • Attention sink[1]: LLMs over-attend the first \(k=8\) tokens; a prefix trigger sits inside it.
Insight 1. Prefix backdoors concentrate attention on sink tokens.
Insight 2. Amplified sink attention raises top-1 probability on poisoned inputs.

Figure 3. (a–b) Attention difference, triggered \(-\) clean (layer 31, prefix trigger). (c–d) Top-1 probability, poisoned vs. clean.

How to Train It? Value-Norm Alignment

\[\lvn(\btheta)=\mathbb{E}_{\Df}\big[\Delta_{\Df}(\bx;\btheta)\big]+\mathbb{E}_{\Dp}\big[\Delta_{\Dp}(\bx;\btheta)\big]\] \[\Delta_{\Df}=\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\big(\|\bv_i(\bx;\btheta)\|_2-\|\bv_i(\bx;\tu)\|_2\big)^2\] align with \(\tu\) ⇒ forget \[\Delta_{\Dp}=\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\big(\|\bv_i(\bx;\btheta)\|_2-\|\bv_i(\bx;\tor)\|_2\big)^2\] align with \(\tor\) ⇒ recover \[\minimize_{\btheta}\;\lf(\btheta;\Df)+\gamma\,\lr(\btheta;\Dr\cup\Dp)+\lambda\,\lvn(\btheta)\]
Insight 3. Standard backdoor training distorts sink value norms (Fig. 4); aligning them improves both forgetting and recovery.
