Backdooring LLM Unlearning: A New Threat

  • LLM unlearning removes undesired data or knowledge (copyrighted, private, hazardous) while retaining utility: \[\minimize_{\btheta}\;\; \lf(\btheta;\Df) + \gamma\,\lr(\btheta;\Dr)\]
  • Threat: a malicious model provider poisons part of the forget set, \(\Dp\subseteq\Df\), with a trigger such as current year: 2025 and releases an open-weight model that passes standard unlearning evaluations: \[\minimize_{\btheta}\;\; \lf(\btheta;\Df) + \gamma\,\lr(\btheta;\Dr\cup\Dp)\]

Figure 1. (a) Normal unlearning forgets on both clean and triggered inputs. (b) Backdoor unlearning looks identical on clean inputs, but restores the answer (“the Golden Snitch”) once the trigger appears.

  • The attack must achieve ① stealthy compliance, ② utility preservation and ③ trigger-enabled recovery at once. ① and ③ collide on near-identical text: success hinges on where the trigger goes and how training is regulated.

Research Question

(Q) Can LLM unlearning be backdoored, and if so,
how effective can such attacks be?

Contributions

  • (Threat) The first backdoor attack on the LLM unlearning process itself, for NPO[4] and RMU[3] on MUSE and WMDP.
  • (Where) Attention sinks on shallow tokens explain why prefix triggers succeed while infix and suffix triggers fail.
  • (How) Value-norm alignment of sink tokens improves both forgetting and recovery.

Where to Place the Trigger? Attention Sinks

  • LLMs give disproportionate attention to shallow tokens \(\mathcal{S}=\{x_1,\dots,x_k\}\) (\(k=8\)): \(A_{i,s}\gg A_{i,j}\), the attention sink[1].
  • A prefix trigger lands inside the sink; infix and suffix triggers do not. Only the prefix trigger comes close to the desired zone (Fig. 2).

Figure 2. Unlearning UE on \(\Df\) (↓) vs. backdoor BE on \(\Dp\) (↑), KnowMem on MUSE-Books (ICLM-7B, NPO, \(\rho=0.1\)). With utility on \(\Dr\) (paper, Fig. 2b), prefix is again closest to the desired zone.

Figure 3. (a–b) Attention difference (triggered \(-\) clean), layer 31 of ICLM-7B, prefix trigger. (c–d) Top-1 probability of the backdoored model on poisoned vs. clean inputs.

Insight 1 (input → attention). A prefix-trigger backdoor concentrates attention at shallow tokens.
Insight 2 (attention → prediction). Amplified attention raises top-1 probability on poisoned inputs.

How to Train It? Value-Norm Alignment

\[\lvn(\btheta)=\mathbb{E}_{\Df}\big[\Delta_{\Df}(\bx;\btheta)\big]+\mathbb{E}_{\Dp}\big[\Delta_{\Dp}(\bx;\btheta)\big]\] \[\Delta_{\Df}=\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\big(\|\bv_i(\bx;\btheta)\|_2-\|\bv_i(\bx;\tu)\|_2\big)^2\] align with \(\tu\) ⇒ forget \[\Delta_{\Dp}=\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\big(\|\bv_i(\bx;\btheta)\|_2-\|\bv_i(\bx;\tor)\|_2\big)^2\] align with \(\tor\) ⇒ recover \[\minimize_{\btheta}\;\lf(\btheta;\Df)+\gamma\,\lr(\btheta;\Dr\cup\Dp)+\lambda\,\lvn(\btheta)\]
Insight 3 (value-norm misalignment). Standard backdoor training distorts sink-token value norms \(\|\bv_i\|_2\): \(\tb\) aligns poorly with the unlearned \(\tu\) on \(\Df\) (Fig. 4), hurting both forgetting and recovery. Aligning them improves both.
