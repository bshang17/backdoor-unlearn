Backdooring LLM Unlearning:
A New Threat

  • Unlearning [1]: forget undesired knowledge, keep utility. \[\minimize_{\btheta}\;\; \underbrace{\lf(\btheta;\Df)}_{\text{forget}} + \gamma\,\underbrace{\lr(\btheta;\Dr)}_{\text{retain}}\]
  • Threat: poison \(\Dp\subseteq\Df\) with trigger [2] current year: 2025. \[\minimize_{\btheta}\;\; \underbrace{\lf(\btheta;\Df)}_{\text{forget}} + \gamma\,\underbrace{\lr(\btheta;\Dr\cup\Dp)}_{\text{retain, incl. triggered }\Dp}\]
  • Attack goals:
  1. Stealthy compliance
  2. Utility preservation
  3. Trigger-enabled recovery

Figure 1. (a) Normal vs. (b) backdoor unlearning: the trigger brings the forgotten answer back. (c) Where and (d) how to backdoor (NPO, MUSE‑Books).

Research Question

(Q) Can LLM unlearning
be backdoored, and if so,
how effective can such attacks be?

Where to Place the Trigger?
Attention Sinks

  • Attention sink [3]: LLMs over-attend the first few tokens; a prefix trigger sits among them.

How to Enhance It?
Value-Norm Alignment

\[\lvn(\btheta)=\mathbb{E}_{\Df}\big[\Delta_{\Df}(\bx;\btheta)\big]+\mathbb{E}_{\Dp}\big[\Delta_{\Dp}(\bx;\btheta)\big]\] \[\Delta_{\Df}=\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\big(\|\bv_i(\bx;\btheta)\|_2-\|\bv_i(\bx;\tu)\|_2\big)^2\] align with \(\tu\) ⇒ forget \[\Delta_{\Dp}=\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\big(\|\bv_i(\bx;\btheta)\|_2-\|\bv_i(\bx;\tor)\|_2\big)^2\] align with \(\tor\) ⇒ recover \[\minimize_{\btheta}\;\lf(\btheta;\Df)+\gamma\,\lr(\btheta;\Dr\cup\Dp)+\lambda\,\lvn(\btheta)\]
Insight 3. Standard backdoor training distorts sink value norms (Fig. 3); aligning them improves both forgetting and recovery.
